# Story Index: Thomas Lee - FE International (M&A Brokerage)

**Founder:** Thomas Lee
**Company:** FE International (Business Acquisition/M&A Platform)
**Revenue Status:** $1B+ in transactions facilitated
**Category:** B2B Services / Marketplace / M&A
**Business Model:** Commission-based brokerage (15%+ down to single digits)
**Stage:** Scaled marketplace operation

---

## 1. The Founder's Journey

**Background:** Started in college (2008-2009) buying and selling domain names, then websites. First deal: $100 → $500 (on credit card). By 2010, graduating college but didn't want a job, so turned side hustle into full-time business. "I was very much self taught."

**Key Characteristics:**
- Self-funded from first deal
- Bootstrap mentality (used credit card)
- Process-focused (not deal-size focused)
- Eventually hired team (2012 onwards)
- Built in NYC high-rise
- Now facilitates $100M+ transactions regularly

---

## 2. Business Model Evolution

**Phase 1: Domain Flipping (2008)**
- Bought domain names
- Sold for slightly more
- Realized domains had value

**Phase 2: Website Flipping (2009-2010)**
- Discovered you could buy established revenue-generating websites
- First deal: $100 → $500 (at end of month after running ads)
- Charged himself with credit card then paid it off with proceeds
- Repeated this $100 → profit cycle until had enough capital

**Phase 3: Course + Book (2010-2011)**
- Wrote book on buying/selling websites (very popular topic then)
- Made $30,000 (reinvested into business)
- People read book → asked: "Can you sell my business?"
- Realized: Could facilitate sales without putting up capital

**Phase 4: Accidental M&A Firm (2011)**
- Started brokering businesses for people
- Added business partner Ismail (ex-investment banker)
- Applied "multi-billion dollar transaction processes" to small deals
- At the time, biggest deal was $30K. Applied institutional M&A processes to it.

**Phase 5: Scaled Operations (2012+)**
- Opened small office
- Hired 2 technical people (server-related work)
- Thomas handled selling
- Realized: Scale by hiring, not by being freelancer
- Moved from $100K to $1M+ deals
- Now regularly $10M+ (8-figure) deals

## 3. Finding Deals & Customers

**Outreach Strategy:**
- Email founders directly (cold outreach)
- Personal, tailored messages (not generic)
- Explain why you think their business is a fit
- Example: "Hey, I'm starting out, I'm looking to acquire a business. Yours looks like a great fit. Are you interested in a conversation?"
- Avoid: Generic "Love what you're building. Can we do a phone call?"

**Trust Building:**
- Small relationships lead to big deals
- Reference from past client: "If I was going to trust anyone with my business, it would be FE International"
- Once proven small deal, bigger deals follow

---

## 4. What Makes a Good Business

**Three Characteristics:**

**1. Evergreen:**
- Offering something that stays interesting forever
- Audience/market is timeless (not a trend)
- Product can change, but customer need stays constant

**2. Recurring Revenue:**
- Do customers come back?
- Not just one-time purchases
- Newsletter model, email outreach, consistent contact
- Repeat business easier to market than finding new customers constantly
- Can be SaaS ($100-$1K/month) or simpler (newsletter, email list)

**3. Growth:**
- Growing businesses worth more
- Buyers pay premium for growth
- Declining or flat business = lower valuation

---

## 5. Valuation & Pricing

**Valuation Method (Small Business):**
- Multiple of PROFIT (not revenue)
- $50K/month revenue with $5K/month expenses = $5K profit
- Still priced at profit multiple, not revenue

**Valuation Range:**
- Average small business: 4-10x annual profit
- Good/growing business: 10x+ annual profit
- Bad business: Below 4x or not sellable
- Depends on: Growth rate, margins, recurring revenue

**Commission Structure:**
- Smaller deals: 15% commission
- Scales down as deal size increases
- Large deals: Single digits
- Example: $100K deal = 15%, $100M deal = much lower %

## 6. Scaling Journey - First Major Milestone

**The Breakthrough Deal:**
- First $10M+ deal (game changer)
- Came from past client referral
- Client said: "I hadn't sold anything that big before, but because they had referral, they trusted us"
- Result: Proved they could handle 8-figure transactions

**Key Insight:**
"The difference between selling a million dollar business and $20 million business isn't really that different. It's slightly more technical, takes a bit more time. But fundamentally, if you know how to sell something for a million, you can sell something for 20."

**Compounding Effect:**
- Once did one 8-figure deal, more came
- Network effect: Investors know FE International
- More deal flow = more practice = better processes

## 7. Data-Driven Decision Making

**Pareto Principle Application:**
When Ismail joined, he analyzed where time was spent:
- 4 different business lines
- 80% of profit came from ONE business line
- Thomas spending 20% of time on that, 80% on the others
- Decision: Stop everything else, focus on the profitable business

**This early focus was critical to company growth.**

---

## 8. Advice for First-Time Buyers

**Starting with $0:**
"Don't necessarily decide it has to be a business. You can find anything to flip."

**Getting Capital:**
1. Get a job for a few days
2. Borrow money
3. Start with something

"$100 vs. $0 = huge difference. Very few people will give you anything for $0."

**Learning Focus (Not Money Focus):**
"You're only really trying to do is learn the process. You're learning how to sell, you're learning how to negotiate."

"Early on, doesn't matter if it's worth your time by the hour. All you're doing is learning."

**Scaling Over Time:**
- Buy for $100, sell for $200 (learn)
- Buy for $1K, sell for $2K (learn)
- Buy for $1M, sell for $2M (apply same skills at scale)
- Buy for $10M, sell for $20M (the process is the same)

**Track Record Matters:**
Investors want to see proof. Show: "I've turned $10K into $20K five times. Will you back me for $1M?"

---

## 9. Key Lessons

**Foundational Principle:**
"You are not going to make your living long term buying things for $100 and selling them for two. You will do if you can apply that same skill to buy for a million and sell for 2 million because all you're really trying to do is learn the process."

**On Building a Business vs. Being a Freelancer:**
"If it's always just you and you never hire any people, it's very difficult to scale."

**On Delegation:**
"One of the first things I learned very early on is I didn't know how to do everything."

**On Hiring:**
"You should focus on the things you are best at. And if you're not the best at it, you should hire people to do that."

## Summary

Thomas Lee took his first $100 deal (domain flip) and built FE International into a $1B+ transaction facilitation business by:

1. **Starting with nothing** - Used credit card, paid it back with profits
2. **Learning the process** - Realized process matters more than deal size
3. **Building in public** - Course/book showed expertise
4. **Hiring complementary skills** - Ismail's investment banking background was crucial
5. **Focusing on what works** - Pareto principle (80/20 rule) applied ruthlessly
6. **Staying data-driven** - Made objective decisions based on profitability, not ego

Key insight: The M&A process is learnable. Same methodology applies whether buying for $100 or $100M. Scale comes from refining the process, not finding bigger deals.

## Actionable Frameworks

**Buyer's Journey:**
1. Decide budget first
2. Identify business model you want (blog, SaaS, etc.)
3. Find founders where they hang out (email + personal outreach)
4. Show specific interest (reference their product/market)
5. Be willing to walk away (confidence is attractive)

**Seller's Advice:**
1. Know your profit multiple (not revenue multiple)
2. Build recurring revenue (buyers pay premiums)
3. Show growth trajectory (growing businesses worth more)
4. Have clean financials (transparency builds trust)
5. Don't price based on feelings (use data)
