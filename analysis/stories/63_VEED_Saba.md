# Story Index: Saba Kanejad - VEED.IO ($10M-$40M+ Revenue)

**Founder:** Saba Kanejad (UK-based, designer → coder → CEO)
**Company:** VEED.IO (browser-based video editing)
**Revenue:** $10M-$40M+ (grew from $0 in ~7 years)
**Model:** SaaS video tool, freemium + paid tiers
**Background:** Designer with cracked Photoshop → self-taught developer
**Strategy:** Relentless execution, brutality against low-quality work, massive SEO

---

## 1. The Background - Childhood Passion Ignited

**Early Years:**
- Grew up in rural area, spent lots of time on internet
- Discovered cracked Photoshop, learned graphics/design
- Pursued design in college and university
- First job: Miserable, wanted to quit immediately (wanted to own time)

**The Spark:**
- Read in free newspaper: Candy Crush makes £1M per day
- Thought: "I could build an app"
- Met Tim (co-founder) a year later through hackathon
- Decided to build something together

---

## 2. The Early Years - Two Years of Nothing

**First Phase (2-3 years):**
- Saba and Tim worked on different app ideas
- Early attempts: Complete failures
- Process: Try idea → fail → learn
- Key question: "Can we get something up and running?"

**The Brutal Reality:**
- Worked in co-working space (shared entry card)
- Hours: 6:30 AM to 8 PM daily
- Gym in middle to stay sane
- Discount food on way home (London half-price deals)
- Every expense nickel-and-dimed: negotiated Crisp Chat down from $100 to $20/month

**The Vision:**
- Knew they wanted to build something in video
- Inspired by Giphy's GIF editor
- Idea: "Can we do browser-based video editing like Giphy does GIFs?"
- Believed: "If others have done it, why can't we?"

**The Catastrophe:**
- 2 years of runway spent with no progress
- Got kicked out of VC office (where they were working)
- Interns quit on the same day
- Had to sell crypto to keep company alive
- Couldn't afford train rides around London
- Friends buying houses, they had zero

**The Turn:**
- Both got jobs as full-time employees (finally had money for normal life)
- Kept working on VEED at night/weekends
- Took HALF their salary monthly, invested into VEED
- Used that money to hire 2 engineers from Upwork
- These engineers proved critical to company growth

---

## 3. The Breakthrough - From 0 to 1M Users

**The Pivot:**
- Stopped focusing on features/perfection
- Started focusing on SEO and growth

**SEO Strategy (Counterintuitive):**
- Problem: Dyslexic, but needed to write landing pages
- Solution: Write landing pages for every keyword people search
- Found ~500 keywords: "trim video", "crop video", etc.
- Created landing page for each
- Made YouTube video for each landing page
- "Absolute brute force"

**The Result:**
- 0 users → 30,000 monthly users in 9 months
- Proof of product-market fit
- Still hadn't charged anyone yet

---

## 4. The Y Combinator Rejection - That Changed Everything

**The Application:**
- Had 30K monthly users + impressive proof of commitment
- Applied to Y Combinator (famous startup accelerator)
- Got interview round
- Felt confident: "We've got this"

**The Rejection:**
- Rejection email: "You don't understand why you haven't charged users"
- Devastating

**The Response (Crazy but Perfect):**
- Implemented feedback over the weekend
- Got 20 paying users
- Emailed YC Monday morning: "We heard you, we fixed it, reconsider us?"
- Second rejection: "No"

**The Twist:**
- Became HUGE driver: "Screw you, we'll do this ourselves"
- Proved that rejection was motivating, not terminal

---

## 5. The Growth Phase - SEO + Brute Force

**Post-Rejection Strategy:**
- Only focus: Make traffic higher than yesterday/week
- Make revenue higher than yesterday/week
- Every 4-6 months: Learn new skill (SEO, then marketing, etc.)

**The SEO Playbook:**
- Build landing page for every search term (500+)
- Record YouTube video explaining the feature
- Rank on both Google and YouTube
- Slow burn, but incredible foundation

**The Growth Curve:**
- 0 → 1M: First year (after finally charging)
- 1M → 2M: 4 months later
- 2M → 3M: 2 months later
- 3M → 6M: 6 months later
- 6M → 40M: Over next years

**The Feeling:**
"It was like off to the races. 0 to 1, 1 to 6. This like proper growth phase of the company going from 10, 20, 30, 40 million in revenue."

---

## 6. The Turning Point - "Screw You" Energy

**Saba's Observation:**
"I don't know, it was like a bit of a middle finger to all the people who didn't want to invest in me or YC turning me down. It was a bit of a, hey, we're good, you know, like, you know, your loss."

**Key Insight:**
Rejection from prestigious source (YC) became fuel instead of stopping point.

---

## 7. The Scaling Challenge - Founder Vulnerability

**The Paradox:**
- Company succeeding dramatically
- Founder increasingly paranoid
- "The bigger you get, the harder it is and you only feel more and more vulnerable"

**The Truth About Building:**
- Not just problem-solving
- People management
- Trying to do what's best for people AND company
- "It's genuinely really, really challenging"

**The Close Calls:**
Company could have failed at:
1. When interns quit
2. When ran out of money
3. When kicked out of office
4. When rejected by YC
5. Multiple other inflection points

**The Secret:**
"Every single time we kept going. And I'm so grateful for all of those learnings. So grateful."

## 10. Key Lessons from Saba's Journey

**Lesson 1: Rejection is fuel if you choose it**
- YC said no
- Response: Build anyway
- Result: $40M+ company

**Lesson 2: Brute force beats luck**
- No shortcuts discovered
- Long hours, consistent effort
- SEO investment paid off for 7 years

**Lesson 3: Speed to market > perfection**
- Worried about code quality
- Shipped anyway
- Could refactor later

**Lesson 4: Founder resilience matters**
- 2 years of nothing = massive mental test
- Most would quit
- Saba kept going

**Lesson 5: Half your salary back into company**
- While working jobs
- Proved faith in the vision
- Hired critical engineers

**Lesson 6: Founder vulnerability is real**
- Success doesn't eliminate doubt
- Bigger company = more pressure
- Paranoia about making wrong decisions

---

## Summary

Saba's VEED.IO journey demonstrates:

1. **Two years of nothing is survivable if you believe**
   - Most founders quit by month 6
   - Saba made it 24 months before breakthrough

2. **Rejection from prestigious sources is often signal, not terminal**
   - YC said no
   - Company became $40M+ anyway
   - Rejection proved he was thinking bigger

3. **SEO + brute force can build $40M+ company**
   - No viral moment
   - No VC injection
   - Just showing up every day for 7 years

4. **Founder doubt doesn't go away with success**
   - Even at $40M+, Saba feels vulnerable
   - Bigger company = harder decisions

5. **Resilience through team**
   - Got through dark times by having co-founder + later employees
   - Having people depending on you is motivation

**For aspiring founders:** Saba's story is a masterclass in persistence. Not inspirational survivor porn—realistic grinding. 2 years of nothing, then 5 years of slow climb, then explosive growth. Most people quit in year 1. Saba made it to year 2 and discovered the breakthrough was right there.
