# Story Index: Lucas Herman - Stage Timer (Countdown Timer SaaS)

**Founder:** Lucas Herman
**Company:** Stage Timer (Live Event Countdown App)
**Revenue Status:** $25,000 MRR ($300K+ ARR)
**Category:** SaaS / Live Event Tools
**Business Model:** Freemium SaaS with premium subscriptions
**Stage:** Profitable, sustainable, family business

---

## 1. The Founder's Journey

**Background & Motivation:**
Lucas Herman is a software developer from Germany who built a "stupidly simple" app that turned into a $25K/month business. He started as a developer in 2007 in high school, riding a bicycle to work at his first development job. After university, he freelanced and worked in startups, always dreaming of building his own product. In November 2020, right as Corona was hitting, he started tinkering on what would become Stage Timer.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Solo founder turned family business (wife joined)
- Deep technical skills (JavaScript, Vue.js, Node.js)
- Used tools he already knew (no learning curve delays)
- Built MVP in 3 days (not months)
- Took 224 days to first dollar (patient with early growth)
- Wife-as-co-founder model (split responsibilities)

---

## 2. Problem Discovery & Validation

**The Personal Discovery:**
While in his friend's studio, Lucas observed the friend using an old Flash app on a laptop to control timers remotely. The friend had to get up, walk to another room, push a button, and walk back. Lucas immediately thought: "Surely there's a better way."

**The Insight:**
"You go to any other business and you just observe people doing their job, and you find that they waste hours and they do things in the most awkward ways that you would have automated long ago." - Simple observation = best business ideas.

**Validation Approach:**
- Posted simple MVP on Reddit in niche subreddit
- Reddit post: 58 upvotes (highly upvoted in small community)
- Reddit users: "I've been waiting for such an app"
- No price attached (removed sales pressure)
- One post per subreddit (not spammy)

---

## 3. Product Development & Iteration

**MVP Philosophy:**
"If I would have used new, unknown technologies, I would have had to learn them, understand them, find out they have limitations I didn't know before. And it was good because I could ship my MVP in three days."

**Technical Stack:**
- JavaScript, Vue.js, Node.js (tools already known)
- No Vercel initially (wanted to avoid new learning)
- Simple tech stack = fast shipping
- Architecture: Clicked button = timer starts counting on screen

**MVP Features:**
- Single feature: Click button, timer counts
- Responsive: Works across devices
- Shareable: Multiple people can see same timer
- Reliable: Handles multiple simultaneous viewers

**Development Timeline:**
- 3 days to MVP
- 224 days to first dollar (incredibly patient)
- Side project in "one hour each evening"
- Built slowly alongside full-time job
- Eventually became full-time (by 2022, made $3,000/month)

---

## 4. Go-to-Market Strategy & Customer Acquisition

**Channel 1: Reddit Organic Seeding**
- Posted in niche video production subreddit
- Message: "Try it out, give me feedback, is this useful?"
- No pricing (reduced friction)
- Result: Direct user feedback, feature requests

**Channel 2: Word of Mouth & Integration Partners**
- 30% of traffic: People recommending to others
- Freemium model: Users bring to events (network effect)
- Product-led growth: "Every link shared has my logo on it"
- Branding on shared timers (Stage Timer logo always visible)

**Channel 3: SEO & Niche Documentation**
- 50% of traffic: Google search
- Strategy: Match existing user searches
- Documentation: "Countdown timer + Stream Deck integration"
- YouTube integration: Video tutorials on integrations
- Target: Specific niche keywords with high intent

**Channel 4: Organic Virality Through Freemium**
- Free tier accessible to anyone
- Freelancers use at events (bring to clients)
- Client/vendor uses product = potentially purchases
- Creates network effects in event production

## 15. Actionable Frameworks & Takeaways

**Idea Discovery Framework:**
1. Observe people working in their environment
2. Notice inefficiencies (buttons they have to walk to press)
3. Ask: "Is there a better way?"
4. Check if anyone solved it better
5. Validate that people will pay for improvement

**Lean Development Framework:**
1. Use tech stack you already know (no learning curve)
2. Build MVP with core feature only (1 button = 1 timer)
3. Ship in days, not months (3-day MVP vs. 3-month "perfect")
4. Don't optimize early (simple JSON files are fine)
5. Iterate based on real usage (market feedback)

**Go-to-Market Framework:**
1. Validate niche with small community first (Reddit)
2. Offer free/freemium to reduce friction
3. Include branding in product (every shared timer has logo)
4. Build documentation for specific use cases (Countdown Timer + Stream Deck)
5. Let network effects work (people bring product to their customers)

**Profitability Framework:**
1. Focus on SaaS (inherent scalability, high margins)
2. Keep fixed costs minimal (cheap servers, few tools)
3. Hire as late as possible (stayed solo for years)
4. Use family/trusted partners (wife as co-founder)
5. Optimize for happiness/sustainability (3 hours/month, $25K/month = 💰)

## Summary

Lucas Herman built a $25K/month, 80%+ margin SaaS business selling countdown timers to event producers. Starting as a weekend project in his friend's studio observation, he validated on Reddit, patiently waited 224 days for first dollar, and built entirely with tools he already knew.

The genius was simplicity: A timer that multiple people can see isn't new, but doing it well for live events at low cost created a defensible niche. The business model was pure freemium virality - users bring the free version to events, organizations like it, they subscribe.

When wife wanted to leave teaching, she joined the business, took over marketing and customer support, and the business scaled to $25K MRR with 80%+ margins (costs just $1,930/month). They now spend 3 hours/month maintaining it while running their main startup.

Most importantly, Lucas proves that a "stupidly simple" idea, executed with patience and lean operations, can create a highly profitable, sustainable business that actually frees the founders from needing venture capital or aggressive growth tactics.
