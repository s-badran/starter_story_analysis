# Story 95: Chris - Wishlist App
## $150K/Year Side Project, 6 Years Building, Solopreneur Lifestyle, 1.1M Users

---

## 1. Founder Background & Journey
**Founder:** Chris
**Starting Point:** University student rejected from bike company job despite enthusiasm; motivated by rejection
**Current Status:** $150K+/year side project turned full-time (quit job 2 months prior), 1.1M users
**Key Characteristic:** Ultra-consistent 6-year marathon builder who maintained day job until product proved sustainable

---

## 2. Problem Discovery & Opportunity Identification
**Motivation (Not Problem):** Rejected from job application despite showing enthusiasm; motivated to prove ability through building

**The Idea Process:**
- Wanted to get into app development
- Initial thought: to-do list app (2M already exist)
- Realization: similar category with less competition needed
- Looked at own life: personally using Excel sheet for Christmas wishlist
- Insight: no nice, pretty wishlist app existed
- Built wishlist app as "nicer version of notes app for sharing wishes with family"

**Validation:** Personal use case (founder was ideal customer) plus market observation (gap in app store)

---

## 3. Product/Service Details & Monetization
**Product:** Wishlist App - simple app to create wishlists, add wishes, share with family

**Core Features:**
1. Create wishlists (with custom names, themes, visibility settings)
2. Add wishes manually or auto-import from retailers
3. Share wishlists with friends/family
4. Track wish fulfillment

**Monetization Model:**
1. **In-App Purchases:** Premium membership (custom images, advanced features)
2. **Affiliate Links:** Auto-convert retailer links (Amazon, etc.) to affiliate links
   - When user opens Amazon product link within app, it converts to affiliate link
   - Commission earned when product purchased through affiliate link
   - Transparent monetization: no product markup, just affiliate commission

**Revenue:** $150K/year ($12.5K/month average, with seasonal variation)

**Seasonal Pattern:** Can multiply revenue by 5 during high season (holidays)

---

## 4. Go-to-Market Strategy & Customer Acquisition
**GTM Approach:** No standard marketing; organic app store growth + word-of-mouth

**Growth Channels:**

**1. App Store Optimization (ASO) – The Core Driver:**
- In-app review prompts at right moments (after wish accomplished)
- Timing: show review request after positive user action (psychological driver)
- Reviews create social proof and ranking signals
- Key metric: maintain high star rating through excellent product

**2. Word-of-Mouth Through Product Design:**
- Sharing feature (users share wishlists with family) = viral distribution loop
- Every share exposes new user to app
- Frictionless experience encourages sharing

**3. No Paid Marketing:**
- "I didn't do any marketing the standard way"
- Relied entirely on organic growth through reviews and sharing

**4. Zero Advertising Budget:**
- Single developer with day job
- No money for ads
- Forced into organic-only strategy (actually turned out to be advantageous)

---

## 5. Business Model & Revenue Streams
**Model:** Freemium mobile app (iOS-focused)

**Revenue Streams:**
1. **In-App Purchases (IAP):** Premium membership ($x/month or $y/year)
2. **Affiliate Commissions:** Retailer links converted to affiliate URLs
   - Amazon affiliate links (primary)
   - Other retailers as users add to wishlists

**Profitability:** ~99% margin (minimal server costs, solo developer)

**Business Sustainability:** Revenue sufficient to replace developer day job; $150K/year vs. full-time salary

---

## 6. Team, Hiring & Scaling Strategy
**Team:** Solopreneur (only Chris)

**Scaling Approach:**
- Built as side project while employed full-time
- No employees or contractors (solo architecture)
- Used day job as stability while building

**Timeline:**
- 2019-2020: 3 years building while at university + full-time job
- 2023: Finished university; got full-time job as app developer
- 2024: Negotiated 4-day week (kept day job but secured Friday for Wishlist)
- 2025: Quit day job after proving product sustainability

**Why Solo:**
- Product simple enough for one developer
- Solopreneur economics better than small team for lifestyle business
- Day job provided stability during ramp-up

---

## 7. Key Operational Decisions & Frameworks
**Decision 1: Start Small with Real Problem**
- "Start with an actual problem either you or someone else experience"
- Founder's own Excel wishlist pain point
- Guarantee: if founder has problem, at least one other person does

**Decision 2: Set Progressive Goals (Not Overnight Success)**
- Initial goal: NOT to make $150K/year
- Initial goal: "Get one random person I don't know to download the app"
- Secondary goal: "Get to 100 users"
- Tertiary goal: "Get to 1M users"
- If started with $150K/year goal, would have quit

**Decision 3: Monetization Comes Last**
- Users first, monetization later
- "Most importantly, just have fun"
- Working full-time anyway, so monetization not urgent

**Decision 4: Build for Users You Know First**
- Share with friends and family
- Ask for reviews (awkward at first but necessary)
- Get early users before going after strangers

**Decision 5: In-App Review Prompts at Right Time**
- Show review request AFTER positive moment (wish accomplished)
- Users predisposed to positive reviews when happy with experience
- Timing increases conversion on review requests

**Decision 6: Keep Day Job Until Proven**
- Allowed 6 years of building without pressure
- Financial stability enabled long-term thinking
- Product proved sustainable before jump
- Negotiated 4-day week to balance both

## 8. Competitive Advantages & Defensible Moats
**1. Simplicity:** Wishlist app is dead simple but perfectly solves specific problem

**2. Emotional Connection:** Associated with holidays and gift-giving (high emotional value)

**3. Network Effects:** Sharing wishlists creates viral loops (every share = potential new user)

**4. First-Mover in Category:** Simple wishlist app (other apps exist but less polished)

**5. Affiliate Revenue Leverage:** Monetization through affiliate links (unique approach)

**6. Low Churn:** Wishlist app has seasonal predictability (high usage around holidays)

**7. Quality and Reliability:** 6 years of solo development = highly polished, reliable product

**8. Founder's Attention:** Solo developer maintains standards and user focus

---

## 9. Lessons Learned & Insights
**Lesson 1: Marathon Over Sprint**
- 6 years of consistent building beats overnight success
- Day job provided stability for long-term thinking
- Marathon mentality allowed perfect product iterations

**Lesson 2: Progressive Goals Prevent Burnout**
- Don't start with "$150K/year goal"
- Start with "$100 goal" or "one download goal"
- Each milestone provides motivation for next milestone

**Lesson 3: Monetization Comes After Product-Market Fit**
- Build for users first
- Revenue follows quality and satisfaction
- Don't force monetization too early

**Lesson 4: Timing Matters for Review Prompts**
- Show review request after positive moment
- Users predisposed to positive reviews when happy
- Simple timing tweak = significant impact

**Lesson 5: Day Job Enabled Product**
- Day job provided financial stability
- Forced discipline (limited hours = focused work)
- No pressure to monetize prematurely

**Lesson 6: Simplicity is Underrated**
- Wishlist app is dead simple
- But simple can be competitive advantage
- Many complex apps outthink their market

**Lesson 7: Use Wintertime for Building**
- In colder months, people spend more time indoors
- Built wishlist primarily during winter
- Summer breaks destroyed momentum (good weather)

**Lesson 8: Work During Holidays**
- Working holidays with other builders combines social + productive
- Motivated environment
- Maintained mental health while being productive

**Advice 2: "Build for problems you experience"**

- Founder as first customer = better product

**Advice 3: "Set progressive goals"**
- Don't start with "$1M goal"
- Start with "one download goal"
- Each milestone enables next one

**Advice 4: "Prioritize users over monetization"**
- Build what users want
- Monetization follows naturally
- Don't force monetization too early

**Advice 5: "Use day job as stability"**
- Day job = financial runway
- Enables long-term thinking
- No pressure to monetize prematurely or cut corners

**Advice 6: "Be honest with employer about side project"**
- Better to be upfront than sneaky

## 12. Comprehensive Summary

**The Playbook:**
