# Story 91: Brett Williams - DesignJoy
## $1.8M/Year Solopreneur, Unlimited Design Subscriptions, 6-Hour Days

---

## 1. Founder Background & Journey
**Founder:** Brett Williams
**Starting Point:** Graphic designer working 9-to-5 for years, designing landing pages, logos, and brand designs for hourly pay
**Current Status:** Nearly $2M/year solopreneur with zero employees, zero contractors, working only 6 hours per day
**Key Characteristic:** Willing to put ego aside, do thousands of reps, and completely embrace a different path than traditional employment or agency scaling

---

## 2. Problem Discovery & Opportunity Identification
**The Discovery:** While employed as a designer, Brett discovered a peculiar website offering unlimited graphic design subscriptions for businesses instead of charging by project or hour. The agency had employees and a brick-and-mortar location, yet operated on a recurring subscription model.

**Market Gap:** Realization that this model could work solo—a designer could build unlimited subscription design services directly to customers via a website without hiring a team or doing sales calls.

**Validation:** The business model itself proved valid by existing. Brett saw immediate proof of concept in real time.

---

## 3. Product/Service Details & Monetization
**Service:** Unlimited graphic design requests for businesses on subscription basis

**Core Features:**
- Clients submit design requests asynchronously via Trello board
- Only one active design request at a time (no concurrent requests)
- Clients cannot change requests mid-project
- Designer delivers work within defined timeline (few days)
- Once completed, client can submit next request

**Revenue Tiers:**
- Started at $449/month
- Progressed to $1,000/month, $3,000/month, $5,000/month
- Now charges up to $8,000/month per client
- Currently maintains ~16 clients at $5,000/month = ~$960K/year (plus premium clients)

**Business Model:** Pure subscription recurring revenue from design services

---

## 4. Go-to-Market Strategy & Customer Acquisition
**GTM Approach:** Demand-based pricing launch strategy

**Launch:** Threw up simple landing page in 12 hours, spent $29, got first paying customer within 24 hours

**Strategy Details:**
- Drastically undercharged initially at $449/month to get immediate customer
- Prioritized speed and momentum over waiting for premium pricing
- Let quality and expertise develop through reps, then raised prices as demand increased
- Attracted ideal clients through consistent delivery and word-of-mouth

**Customer Profile:** Businesses that view design as non-negotiable competitive advantage, prefer subscription certainty over project-by-project chaos

---

## 5. Business Model & Revenue Streams
**Primary Revenue:** Design subscription services ($1M-$2M annually)

**Secondary Revenue (29% of income):** Information products
- Scribbles: Downloadable design template collection ($4.99, 25,000+ downloads)
- Productize Yourself: Course teaching designers how to build productized services (significantly higher price point)

**Margins:** Nearly 100% on core business (solo operator, minimal fixed costs beyond software subscriptions and design tools)

---

## 6. Team, Hiring & Scaling Strategy
**Team:** Zero employees, zero contractors (solopreneur by design)

**Scaling Approach:** NOT through hiring staff—through boundaries and operational efficiency

**Scaling Mechanism:** Boundaries protect time and enable profitability
- Asynchronous Trello board requests prevent constant calls/meetings
- One active request limit prevents scope creep and request volume explosion
- Written request requirements reduce miscommunication and revision cycles
- Client self-filtering (difficult requirements = more considered requests)

**Why No Team:** Brett explicitly chose not to hire because:
- Would lose solopreneur income advantage
- Would sacrifice quality control and personal satisfaction
- Would introduce management overhead that kills profitability
- Would reduce time available for creative work (his passion)

---

## 7. Key Operational Decisions & Frameworks
**Decision 1: Demand-Based Pricing Framework**
- Start underpriced to get momentum and first customers
- Generate thousands of reps and learning cycles
- Let expertise and quality improve naturally over time
- Raise prices as demand increases and reputation grows
- Result: Price multiplier effect (449 → 8,000 = 18x increase)

**Decision 2: Boundaries as Scaling Tool**
- Set clear constraints that protect time and maintain quality
- Create friction that self-selects better clients
- Asynchronous workflows reduce coordination overhead
- More considered requests mean fewer revisions and faster delivery

**Decision 3: Focus on High-Impact Services**
- Design is visually critical to business success (website first impression matters)
- Clients recognize immediate business impact and justify premium pricing
- Low-touch execution possible due to design nature (build once, minimal maintenance)

**Decision 4: Solopreneur Economics**
- Reject scaling through team/hiring
- Optimize for owner income, quality, lifestyle
- Work 6 hours/day by serving fewer clients at premium prices
- Generate additional income through info products that leverage existing knowledge

---

## 8. Competitive Advantages & Defensible Moats
**1. Speed & Turnaround:** Delivers 48-hour turnarounds (vs. weeks for agencies or uncertain timelines for freelancers)

**2. Simplicity & Predictability:** Subscription model removes per-project uncertainty; clients know exactly what they're paying and getting

**3. Quality at Scale:** Years of design expertise combined with specialized focus creates objectively better output than generalist designers

**4. Professionalism & Reliability:** Proven track record of reliable delivery vs. unreliable Upwork/Fiverr lottery

**5. Hybrid Positioning:** Splits the difference between expensive agencies and cheap freelancers—best of both for ideal customers

**6. Personal Brand & Reputation:** Build-in-public approach creates network effects and referral flow

---

## 9. Lessons Learned & Insights
**Lesson 1: Ego Sacrifice = Acceleration**
- Willingness to massively underprice and do thousands of reps is the secret
- Most people won't do this; they want to monetize immediately
- Reps turn Brett into a world-class designer faster than traditional employment

**Lesson 2: Boundaries Scale Better Than Hiring**
- Setting clear constraints actually increases profitability
- Self-selecting client base improves through boundaries (better clients prefer clarity)
- Reduces operational complexity and management overhead

**Lesson 3: Product-Market Fit Matters More Than Revenue**
- High-impact services command premium pricing
- If clients desperately need it, pricing is secondary concern
- Design quality that drives business results justifies any reasonable subscription price

**Lesson 4: Solopreneur Economics Beats Agency Scaling**
- Can generate $2M/year without employees
- Preserve quality, creativity, and lifestyle
- Works for specific service types (high-impact, relatively low-touch)

**Lesson 5: Information Products Are Leverage**
- 29% of income from content with zero ongoing time investment
- Once product is built, revenue flows passively
- Knowledge-based leverage is underutilized by most service providers

**Success Factor 2:** Founder willingness to underprice and iterate—most won't do this; it's Brett's secret weapon

**Success Factor 3:** Exceptional execution—design quality that creates visible business impact for clients

**Success Factor 4:** Boundary-driven operations—enabled profitability without hiring or team complexity

**Success Factor 5:** Premium customer selection—attracted clients who deeply value quality and reliability over price shopping

**Success Factor 6:** Lifestyle optimization—prioritized owner satisfaction over hyper-growth

## 11. Founder Advice for Others
**Advice 1: "Be willing to put your ego aside"**
- Start underpriced if it means getting momentum
- Do thousands of reps even if you're basically working for free initially
- Quality and expertise develop through repetition, not osmosis

**Advice 2: "Prioritize speed over perfection at launch"**
- Get landing page live quickly, even if simple
- Revenue and traction validate the concept better than planning
- Customers force you to improve what matters most

**Advice 3: "Set clear boundaries early"**
- Don't say yes to unlimited scope
- Protect your time through operational constraints
- Better clients actually prefer clarity and structure

**Advice 4: "Pick a service with inherent high impact"**
- Not all productized services work equally well
- Choose something where output directly affects customer revenue/success
- Design, copy, and strategy typically work better than low-impact services

**Advice 5: "Avoid the hiring trap"**
- Hiring doesn't always increase owner income
- Often introduces complexity that kills profitability
- Sometimes solo operation is the most profitable path
