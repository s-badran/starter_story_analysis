# Story 98: Mark Liu - ShipFast
## $1.5M/Year Solopreneur, 30 Failed Startups, Build-in-Public Founder, Founder Despair to Success

---

## 1. Founder Background & Journey
**Founder:** Mark Liu
**Starting Point:** Family of engineers, broke, living with parents, $10/hour waiter
**Journey:** Failed 30+ startups over 5+ years, lost everything during COVID, nearly suicidal, got fired, discovered ShipFast
**Current Status:** $80K-$135K/month MRR, $1.5M/year solopreneur
**Key Characteristic:** Perseverance through extreme failure, founder who failed 30 times before succeeding

---

## 2. Problem Discovery & Opportunity Identification
**The Long Failure Journey:**
1. **Failed Startup #1:** "Tinder for sports lovers" - spent 1 year, $0 revenue, humiliated
2. **Failed Startup #2:** AI startup with VC funding - spent 6 months, $0 customers
3. **Failed Startup #3:** Physical business serving COVID lockdowns - grew to $4K MRR, then COVID destroyed everything
4. **Mental Crisis:** Lost business, got married, moved back to France broke, started crying, nearly suicidal
5. **Day Job:** Took $9K/month job as software engineer to stabilize

**The Turning Point:**
- Got fired from day job (after 6 months)
- Realized: having job didn't fulfill him anymore
- Decided: "I'm going to be an entrepreneur again, but this time differently"

**The New Approach:**
- No spending months on products (learned from failures)
- No raising money (learned from failures)
- No hiring employees (learned from failures)
- Build small projects, ship fast, test market
- Share publicly (build-in-public)

**The ShipFast Discovery:**
- Built code base after shipping 10-15 projects
- Realized: was doing same landing page + payment button + email sequence every project
- Thought: "Why not package this boilerplate for other developers?"
- Expected: maybe make $100
- Reality: $500 in 2 hours (couldn't believe it)
- Next month: $40K

## 3. Product/Service Details & Monetization
**Product:** ShipFast - Developer boilerplate for fast shipping

**Core Value:** Starter codebase for developers to launch projects faster
- Landing page generator included
- Payment setup (Stripe integration)
- Email sequences pre-built
- Deploy-ready architecture

**Customer Base:** Developers, solopreneurs, founders wanting to ship fast

**Pricing:** Premium pricing (developers willing to pay for faster development)

**Revenue:**
- Month 1: $40K
- Month 2: $65K (Black Friday spike)
- Stabilized: $50K-$85K/month
- Yearly: $1.5M+

## 4. Go-to-Market Strategy & Customer Acquisition
**GTM Strategy: Build-in-Public + Viral Content**

**Phase 1: Build Projects Publicly**
- Built and shipped 10-15 projects in rapid succession
- Each project documented and shared publicly
- Twitter updates, TikTok-style skits showing building
- Skit examples: "On a podcast with Joe Rogan," "In a movie with Leonardo DiCaprio"
- Humorous, self-deprecating content that resonated
- People felt founder was having fun

**Phase 2: Free Tools Marketing**
- Built free tools: logo generator, template builder, etc.
- Free tools drive traffic to ShipFast product
- Banners in free tools promote ShipFast
- Free tools also content for distribution

**Phase 3: YouTube Episode**
- Proper YouTube episode about shipping fast
- Later added revenue from platform exposure

**Network Effects:**
- Each product built → more social content
- Social content → more audience
- More audience → more awareness when ShipFast launched

## 7. Key Operational Decisions & Frameworks
**Decision 1: Learn from Failures, Don't Repeat**
- Spent months on products before: now ships fast
- Raised money before: now bootstraps
- Hired employees before: now solo
- Each failure taught specific lesson incorporated into new approach

**Decision 2: Build-in-Public Over Stealth**
- Creates audience alongside product
- Mitigates risk if product fails
- Next product has audience/distribution ready
- Builds personal brand as side effect

**Decision 3: Viral Content via Personality**
- Skit format (Joe Rogan, Leonardo DiCaprio) resonated
- People connected with founder's personality
- Humorous self-deprecation beat polished marketing
- Authenticity > perfection

**Decision 4: Free Tools as Distribution**
- Logo generator, template builder drive traffic
- Free tools get organic traffic
- Banners convert free tool users to paid product
- Creates content opportunities simultaneously

**Decision 5: Ship MVP, Then Iterate**
- Don't overthink features
- Get product to market fast
- Let market feedback guide iterations
- Removed expectations (crucial for motivation)

**Decision 6: Focus on Painkiller, Not Vitamin**
- Shifted to building painkillers (solve real problems)
- Early projects were vitamins (nice-to-have)
- Painkiller products have clearer value prop and easier sells
- Users want relief from pain

**Decision 7: Multi-Income Strategy**
- Multiple products generating revenue
- Portfolio approach reduces risk
- Best performers get more attention
- Failed projects teach lessons for next

## 9. Lessons Learned & Insights
**Lesson 1: Failure Teaches More Than Success**
- 30 failures embedded deep lessons
- Each failure prevented specific mistake in future
- Most people quit before learning; Mark persevered

**Lesson 2: Ship Fast Over Perfect**
- Months building = zero revenue
- Days shipping = revenue feedback
- Speed > perfection in product development

**Lesson 3: Build-in-Public Reduces Risk**
- Build next product while audience watching first
- Audience becomes distribution for next launch
- Mitigates risk of product failure

**Lesson 4: Painkiller > Vitamin**
- Painkillers solve real problems (people buy)
- Vitamins are nice-to-have (people don't buy)
- User emotion (pain relief) = purchasing motivation

**Lesson 5: Personality Beats Polished Marketing**
- Authentic founder stories resonate
- Humorous self-deprecation > corporate marketing
- People buy from people they like

**Lesson 6: Free Tools Drive Paid Product Traffic**
- Free tools get organic traffic
- Banners/upsells convert free tool users
- Creates content distribution simultaneously

**Lesson 7: Portfolio > Single Product**
- Multiple projects reduce risk
- Some succeed, some teach lessons
- Portfolio approach prevents desperation if one fails

**Lesson 8: Remove Expectations**
- "Maybe we'll make $100" = zero pressure
- Zero pressure enabled creativity
- Shipped product relaxed (went skating)
- Success felt surprising vs. disappointing
