**Founder:** Thomas Desmond (Full-time indie hacker)
**Company:** UNID (Product launch platform)
**Revenue:** $10K/month ($120K+ ARR)
**Model:** Platform to showcase tech products (competitor to Product Hunt)
**Background:** Developer who failed 30+ projects before success
**Strategy:** Iterate, timing, positioning, finding the right market moment

---

## 2. Why Most Projects Failed - Five Core Reasons

**Reason 1: Giving Up Too Early**
- People launch, get zero sales, quit after week/two
- Reality: Products don't work overnight
- Solution: Stay consistent, iterate

**Reason 2: Unclear Purpose**
- Landing pages look beautiful but message unclear
- If headline doesn't explain value, no scrolling
- Users leave immediately

**Reason 3: Loss of Momentum**
- Build in public = momentum
- Every Twitter post = reminder in feed
- Stop talking for few weeks = restart from zero
- Classic mistake

**Reason 4: "Build it and they will come"**
- Thomas hates this phrase
- Wrong: No matter how good product, won't work without marketing
- Right: Must talk about it, market it, promote it

**Reason 5: Timing**
- If fails in January doesn't mean fails in July
- Don't give up just because timing is off
- Stay consistent

## 3. The Winning Idea - UNID

**Original Concept:**
- Started as simple directory for frontend tools
- Got minimal traction ($200/month at best)

**The Pivot:**
- Noticed drama around Product Hunt
- Indie hackers complaining: Can't get featured
- Product Hunt only featuring big products/audiences
- Market signal: Opportunity for alternative

**The Timing:**
"If I had pivoted a year before I did, it could have been a huge failure. I took advantage of drama around product hunt."

**Translation:**
- Timing was RIGHT (community frustrated)
- Execution (building alternative) happened at right moment
- Same idea, wrong time = failure

## 5. Ideas That Make a Business

**What Makes GOOD Ideas:**

**Signal 1: You Know How to Sell It**
- Either: Know the market
- Or: Have distribution channel
- Or: Have marketing idea
- GUM Affiliates failed: Didn't know market, didn't know how to reach Gumroad sellers
- UNID worked: Knew indie hackers, knew Twitter, knew how to reach them

**Signal 2: Competitors Exist**
- "If no competitors, there's no market"
- Having competitors = proof of demand
- Creates market validation
- 99% of ideas are not unique
- Focus on existing market, execute better

**Counter-intuitive:**
- No competitors = probably no market
- Not Steve Jobs creating new market (almost impossible)
- If no one wants it, no one wants yours either

**What Makes BAD Ideas:**
- No distribution plan
- No way to reach customers
- No competitors (no proof of market)

## 11. The Tension Between Originality and Execution

**Common Mistake:**
- People think they need unique idea
- Reality: Execution in competitive market > mediocre execution in "unique" market

**Thomas' Evidence:**
- Built Product Hunt competitor
- Not original
- But: Better timing, better execution, better positioning
- Result: $10K/month

**Counter to conventional wisdom:**
"You need a unique idea" = often wrong
"You need to execute in existing market" = often right

## Summary

Thomas demonstrates:

1. **30 failures can teach more than 1 success**
   - Pattern recognition from failures
   - Know what doesn't work
   - Eventually know what does

2. **Timing beats originality**
   - Same idea (launch platform) at different times
   - Wrong time: fails
   - Right time: succeeds

3. **Competition is validation**
   - Competitors = market exists
   - No competitors = probably no market
   - Build better in existing market vs inventing new market

4. **Momentum matters enormously**
   - Stop talking = restart from zero
   - Consistency = compound effect

5. **Clarity of purpose essential**
   - Unclear positioning = no traction
   - Positioning clarity = market understands value

**For aspiring founders:** Thomas' story is antidote to "fail fast" culture. His failures weren't wasted - they were research. 30 failed products taught him patterns. UNID wasn't first attempt, was #31. But it succeeded because of everything learned before.
